# Hosted image-embedding APIs with a free tier, for card scanning

Research note, 2026-10-06 (all pages accessed 2026-10-06). Question: instead of a self-hosted Python sidecar (CLIP/DINOv2), can the backend call a hosted image-embedding API with a free tier to (a) embed about 12,000 catalog images once and (b) embed about 300 user photos per month, for nearest-neighbour search in pgvector on Neon? Only provider-owned pages are cited as evidence. Anything not found on a primary page is marked UNVERIFIED. Several provider pages are JavaScript-rendered or were read through a summarising fetcher, so the exact figures below should be re-checked in each dashboard before committing.

## Bottom line

- **Top pick: Voyage AI `voyage-multimodal-3.5` (now served from MongoDB Atlas, `ai.mongodb.com`).** The free allowance is 150 billion image pixels plus 200M text tokens, which covers the 12k batch about 6 to 16 times over, it accepts public image URLs and base64 over plain HTTPS JSON, and 1024/512/256-dim Matryoshka vectors fit a pgvector HNSW index. Catches: it needs an Atlas account, the free-trial rate limits are UNVERIFIED, and by default submitted data may be used for model training unless you opt out (opt-out needs a payment method on file).
- **Runner-up: Google `gemini-embedding-2` (Gemini API).** Image embeddings are free of charge on the Gemini API free tier with no billing required, MRL dims 128 to 3072 (use 1536 or 768), paid fallback is only about $1.44 for the whole catalog. Catches: free-tier content is used to improve Google products and may be read by human reviewers (the paid tier is not), the free RPM/RPD numbers are not published on a public page (UNVERIFIED), and only PNG/JPEG are accepted (convert WebP).
- **Coverage:** Voyage and Gemini each credibly cover both (a) the 12k batch and (b) about 300 scans per month inside their free allowances. Cohere (trial key), Jina (token grant size not published), Hugging Face, Replicate and Cloudflare (no public image-embedding model) do not, or cannot be confirmed to.
- **Accuracy is UNMEASURED for all of them.** None of these providers publishes a trading-card or near-duplicate image-to-image benchmark. Run the spike below before building anything.
- OpenAI and Anthropic offer no image-embedding endpoint (confirmed in their docs, section 3.10).

## 2. Dimension and index note

pgvector's own README: "`vector` - up to 2,000 dimensions", "`halfvec` - up to 4,000 dimensions" for indexing (https://github.com/pgvector/pgvector, README, accessed 2026-10-06). At 12k rows an HNSW index is optional anyway (an exact scan over 12k vectors is cheap), so the cap mostly matters for the default dims of Voyage (2048 option), Gemini (3072 default) and Cohere v5 (2048 default). All three support truncating to a smaller size, and Gemini auto-normalises truncated dims ("auto-normalizes truncated dimensions"). For Voyage, re-normalise after client-side truncation as its docs show.

## 3. Candidates

### 3.1 Voyage AI (MongoDB Atlas Embedding API)

- **Image embeddings:** yes. `voyage-multimodal-3.5` (text, images, video; 1024 default, 256/512/2048 Matryoshka) and legacy `voyage-multimodal-3` (1024). https://www.mongodb.com/docs/voyageai/models/multimodal-embeddings/ and https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/embeddings (model table), accessed 2026-10-06. Docs say it processes inputs through a single backbone "unlike CLIP". Image-to-image similarity is not mentioned anywhere I read; the same model embeds both sides, so mechanically it works, but the training emphasis is text-to-image and document-screenshot retrieval (blog.voyageai.com/2026/01/15/voyage-multimodal-3-5/, evaluated on ViDoRe, ViDoRe v2, MIRACL-VISION).
- **Provider change to note:** the API now lives at `https://ai.mongodb.com/v1/multimodalembeddings`, with keys created in a MongoDB Atlas project ("AI Model APIs"). Old `api.voyageai.com` endpoints are documented as the pre-migration path (https://www.mongodb.com/docs/voyageai/tutorials/migrate-to-atlas/). No deadline for the old endpoint was stated (UNVERIFIED).
- **Free tier:** "200 million free tokens for each model. The multimodal models also include 150,000,000,000 free pixels" (https://www.mongodb.com/docs/voyageai/management/rate-limits/, https://www.mongodb.com/docs/voyageai/management/billing/). The billing page describes a "Free trial" level and says you must add a payment method to reach Usage Tier 1. Whether a card is needed to sign up and use the free allowance is not stated explicitly (UNVERIFIED); Atlas sign-up itself is described as free. The allowance is a lifetime grant per model, not monthly (the docs say "the first" 150B pixels). Rate limits for the free-trial level are not documented separately (UNVERIFIED); the Tier 1 table lists `voyage-multimodal-3.5` at 2,000,000 TPM and 2,000 RPM. Free tokens do not apply to the Batch API (https://docs.voyageai.com/docs/pricing).
- **12k batch math:** billing is per pixel; images under 50,000 px are charged as 50K px and images over 2M px are downsampled and charged as 2M px (billing page). Worst case 12,000 x 2M = 24B pixels, 16% of the 150B allowance, so at least 6 full re-embeds fit. Tokens: 560 px = 1 token, so a 2M px image is about 3,571 tokens and 12k images is at most about 43M tokens. A request is capped at 320,000 total tokens and 1,000 inputs, so about 90 max-size images per request and about 135 requests. At the Tier 1 limit of 2M TPM that is about 22 minutes at the very most. At a realistic 0.7M px per image it is about 15M tokens, about 8 minutes.
- **300 scans per month:** 300 x at most 2M px = 0.6B px per month, i.e. 0.4% of the lifetime allowance per month; resize the phone photo to about 1 MP before upload anyway.
- **Paid price:** $0.60 per 1B pixels, $0.12 per 1M text tokens. Examples from the billing page: 1000x1000 px = $0.0006 per image ($0.60 per 1K), 2000x2000 px or larger = $0.0012 per image ($1.20 per 1K). Worst case 12k images = $14.40. https://www.mongodb.com/docs/voyageai/management/billing/.
- **Input constraints:** up to 16M px and 20 MB per image; PNG, JPEG, WEBP, GIF; `image_url` (public URL) or `image_base64` (data URL `data:image/png;base64,...`); up to 1,000 inputs per request, 32,000 tokens per input, 320,000 total tokens per request. The docs describe interleaved text/image inputs, so a content array with several items is probably fused into one embedding (UNVERIFIED); send one image per input. https://docs.voyageai.com/reference/multimodal-embeddings-api, accessed 2026-10-06. We can send R2 public URLs for the catalog and base64 for scan photos.
- **Privacy:** "MongoDB can collect the data that you send to the Atlas Embedding and Reranking API and use it to improve Voyage AI models", managed per organization in Atlas settings (https://www.mongodb.com/docs/voyageai/management/model-training-data/). The older Voyage FAQ says customers can opt out for "zero-day retention", but "you must have a payment method on file and be an organization Admin" (https://docs.voyageai.com/docs/faq). The default setting on the Atlas side is UNVERIFIED. Region/data-processing notes: a "geography" scoped endpoint exists per the quickstart text ("keys scoped to a geography use that geography's endpoint"), details UNVERIFIED. **Flag: users' phone photos go to a third party that may train on them unless we add a card and opt out.**
- **Latency:** not documented in the pages read (UNVERIFIED).
- **API shape:** plain HTTPS JSON `POST /v1/multimodalembeddings` with `Authorization: Bearer`, body `{"inputs":[{"content":[{"type":"image_url","image_url":"..."}]}],"model":"voyage-multimodal-3.5","input_type":null,"output_dimension":1024}`. No SDK needed. Response: `data[].embedding`, `usage.image_pixels`. For image-to-image use `input_type` null on both sides (the `query`/`document` values prepend text prompts, which are meaningless for image-only input).
- **Suitability for cards:** general-purpose multimodal embedder tuned for document and text-to-image retrieval, not a CLIP or DINO-style visual-similarity model. No card or fine-grained benchmark published. UNMEASURED.

### 3.2 Google Gemini API (`gemini-embedding-2`)

- **Image embeddings:** yes. `gemini-embedding-2` (stable, April 2026 update) maps "text, images, video, audio, and documents into a unified embedding space"; `gemini-embedding-001` is text-only (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/embeddings, accessed 2026-10-06). Output 128 to 3072 dims via Matryoshka (recommended 768, 1536, 3072; default 3072). It auto-normalises truncated dims. Image-to-image similarity is not discussed; it is mechanically supported by the shared space.
- **Free tier:** the pricing page lists Standard tier "Free of charge" for all input types including image, and Batch tier not available on free (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing). The rate-limits page says the Free tier needs only an "Active project or free trial", with no billing requirement, and tells you to check AI Studio for your actual per-model RPM/TPM/RPD (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits). The exact free-tier embedding limits are not on a public page (UNVERIFIED). A web-search summary mentioned 100 RPM and 1,000 RPD from a user report, which I treat as unofficial. Limits are per project and RPD resets at midnight Pacific.
- **12k batch math:** images are about 258 tokens each (search summary of Google docs; the pricing page's $0.00012 per image at $0.45 per 1M tokens implies about 267), so about 3.1M tokens for 12k images. If the free limit were 1,000 requests per day it takes 12 days (UNVERIFIED limit); at 100 RPM it takes 2 hours. One image per `embedContent` call (see constraints).
- **300 scans per month:** negligible under any plausible daily limit.
- **Paid price:** $0.45 per 1M image tokens, $0.00012 per image standard, $0.225 per 1M and $0.00006 per image batch. Worst case 12k images: $1.44 standard or $0.72 batch. https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing.
- **Input constraints:** max 6 images per request, PNG and JPEG listed; passing several images in one `contents` produces "one aggregated embedding for all inputs", so embed one image per request (or use `batchEmbedContents`, which has no free tier). No explicit size limit documented. Base64 via `inline_data`; no public-URL input shown in the example. WebP catalog originals must be converted to PNG or JPEG before upload. REST: `POST https://generativelanguage.googleapis.com/v1beta/models/gemini-embedding-2:embedContent` with `x-goog-api-key`.
- **Privacy:** Gemini API Additional Terms: on Unpaid Services Google "uses the content you submit ... to provide, improve, and develop Google products", "human reviewers may read, annotate, and process your API input and output" (disconnected from the account/API key), and "Do not submit sensitive, confidential, or personal information to the Unpaid Services". Paid Services: "Google doesn't use your prompts or responses to improve our products", logs kept for a limited time only for abuse detection. For EEA, Switzerland and UK end users only Paid Services may be used (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms). The pricing page "Used to improve our products" row is Yes for free, No for paid. **Flag: on the free tier, user phone photos can be used for product improvement and human review. Enabling billing (a card) flips this to the paid terms, and at 300 scans per month plus a $1.44 re-embed the actual bill would be pennies.**
- **Latency:** not documented in the pages read (UNVERIFIED).
- **Suitability for cards:** generalist multimodal model. Cohere's Embed 5 launch post claims Embed 5 Pro beats Gemini Embedding 2 on fused text-image retrieval (82.3 vs 61.3 averaged over five datasets), which is a competitor claim on document retrieval, not cards. Nothing published on fine-grained image-to-image. UNMEASURED.
- **Vertex AI variant:** Google Cloud also exposes multimodal embeddings through Vertex (GCP billing account needed). I could not extract its model, dims or pricing from the pages fetched, so it is UNVERIFIED and not recommended over the Gemini API for this size.

### 3.3 Jina AI Embeddings API

- **Models and image support:** current list on https://jina.ai/embeddings/ (accessed 2026-10-06): `jina-embeddings-v5-text-small` (1024) and `-nano` (768) are text-only; `jina-embeddings-v5-omni-small` (1.74B params, 1024 dims) and `-omni-nano` (768) handle text, image, audio, video, PDF; `jina-embeddings-v4` (3.8B, text and image); `jina-clip-v2` (865M, text and image). Matryoshka: v5-omni-small supports 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 768 down from 1024 (https://jina.ai/models/jina-embeddings-v5-omni-small/, released 2026-05-07, built on a SigLIP2 Large vision encoder). `jina-clip-v2` truncates 1024 down to 64 and uses 512x512 input (https://huggingface.co/jinaai/jina-clip-v2). The v5-omni image encoder is a frozen add-on to the text model, the page does not claim image-to-image retrieval, only cross-modal.
- **Free tier:** a free key gets 100 RPM and 100,000 TPM on the Embedding API (https://jina.ai/api-dashboard/rate-limit and https://jina.ai/embeddings/); no credit card required to start (embeddings page). "New users get an auto-generated API key with free tokens ... Once the free tokens are consumed, you can purchase additional tokens", but the number of free tokens is not stated on any page I could read (UNVERIFIED; a search snippet said "a million tokens", not on the primary text I fetched). Keys do not expire.
- **12k batch math (token costs for a 600x600 image from the embeddings page):** v5-omni-small about 363 tokens, v5-omni-nano about 362, v4 about 4,840, clip-v2 about 16,000. Totals for 12k images: omni-small 4.4M tokens (44 minutes at 100K TPM, but only if the free grant is at least 4.4M); v4 58M tokens (about 9.7 hours); clip-v2 192M tokens (about 32 hours). So only the v5-omni models are realistic, and then only if the free grant is large enough, otherwise you buy tokens.
- **300 scans per month:** 300 x 363 = about 109K tokens per month on v5-omni-small, small against any grant.
- **Paid price:** "pricing is based on total tokens processed"; per-token price is not on the pages I fetched (UNVERIFIED; a search snippet indicated about $0.045 to $0.05 per 1M tokens).
- **Input constraints:** images up to 5 MB, accepts public URLs or base64 (jina-clip-v2 card says image URLs; base64 UNVERIFIED), batch via the `input` array. API: `POST https://api.jina.ai/v1/embeddings`, plain JSON, `Authorization: Bearer`.
- **Privacy:** "We never use your API requests, inputs, or outputs to train our embedding, reranker, or any other models." (https://jina.ai/embeddings/). Retention period and region UNVERIFIED. Model weights are CC BY-NC 4.0, so self-hosting commercially is not free, the hosted API is the licensed route.
- **Latency:** the embeddings page has an average-latency column, but I could not read the value for the Embedding API (UNVERIFIED).
- **Suitability for cards:** `jina-clip-v2` is a CLIP-style general model (the card cites about 3% improvement over v1 on text-image and text-text retrieval, no image-to-image numbers). v5-omni is a SigLIP2 encoder wired to a text LLM. Nothing for fine-grained retrieval. UNMEASURED.

Embed limits on trial: 5 image inputs per minute (production 400 per minute), text 2,000 per minute (https://docs.cohere.com/docs/rate-limits). Cohere's pricing FAQ: "API calls made from a Trial API key are free. However, trial keys are rate limited and are not permitted to be used for production or commercial purposes." (https://cohere.com/pricing). Whether the app counts as commercial is the owner's call, but a trial key cannot back a production feature under those terms.

- **Input constraints:** `POST https://api.cohere.com/v2/embed`, `input_type: "image"` with `images` as base64 data URLs (`data:image/png;base64,...`); PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF; 5 MB per image in the embeddings guide, v5 allows up to 96 images per request and 20 MB payload; images over 2,458,624 px are downsampled. No public-URL input. https://docs.cohere.com/docs/embeddings and https://docs.cohere.com/reference/embed.
- **Privacy:** an excerpt of the Terms of Use indicates Cohere may access and use customer data to improve the service by default; I read this through a summariser and the exact opt-out path is UNVERIFIED. https://cohere.com/terms-of-use.

## 4. Risks and open questions

- **Accuracy on holo/glare/perspective phone photos is UNMEASURED for every candidate.** All the strong candidates (Voyage, Gemini, Cohere) are VLM-style embedders benchmarked on text-to-image and document retrieval. Image-to-image retrieval of the same card under glare and angle is a different task, and none of the providers publish such a benchmark. Expect that top-1 may be much worse than a purpose-built visual-similarity model, and that cropping/perspective correction on the client may matter more than the model choice.
- **Privacy of user photos.** Voyage may train on submitted data unless you add a card and opt out; Gemini free tier uses content for product improvement with possible human review (paid does not); Cohere's terms point to default data use for improvement; Jina states it never trains. Phone photos can include a face or background. Mitigations: crop to the card on the client before upload, use paid terms (a card on file is enough for Google and Voyage, and the spend stays near zero), tell users in the privacy notice.
- **Free-tier changes over time.** Voyage moved from its own platform to MongoDB Atlas keys; Cohere launched Embed 5 six days ago; Gemini free limits are viewable only in AI Studio and are not guaranteed ("specified rate limits are not guaranteed and actual capacity may vary"). A free allowance can be cut. The batch is one-shot, so that risk is mostly on the scan path and on any future re-embed.
- **Vendor lock-in and re-embed cost.** Embeddings from different models are not comparable, so changing model means re-embedding all 12k catalog images and storing a `model` and `dims` column next to the vector. Worst-case paid re-embed cost is small: Voyage up to $14.40, Gemini $1.44 ($0.72 batch), Replicate about $3.72. The real cost is engineering time and the free allowance being a lifetime grant (Voyage) rather than renewable.
- **Availability and latency.** A third-party outage breaks scanning; latency is not documented by Voyage or Google, so measure round-trip from Railway in the spike. Keep a fallback of "no match, search by name" in the UI.
- **Rate-limit unknowns on the free trial** (Voyage free-trial RPM/TPM, Gemini RPM/RPD, Jina grant size). The spike's batch step doubles as the measurement.
- **WebP and URL handling.** Gemini needs PNG/JPEG base64 (no URL in the example), Cohere needs base64, Voyage accepts R2 URLs directly, Jina accepts URLs. Pick the endpoint that lets us stream R2 objects with the least conversion, but remember Neon and Railway egress.
- **Doubles of the same card.** Reprints and variants (holo vs non-holo, same art) will be near-neighbours in any embedding. Plan to show top-5 for the user to confirm, not an automatic top-1.

## 5. Suggested spike (half a day)

1. Pick 200 catalog cards (include 20 holo/reverse-holo and 10 reprints with the same art). Take or collect 30 real phone photos of those cards, mixed lighting, some glare, some tilted, some rough crops. Keep the ground-truth card id per photo.
2. Embed the 200 catalog PNG/WebP images and the 30 photos with each of: Voyage `voyage-multimodal-3.5` (1024 dims, `input_type` null), Gemini `gemini-embedding-2` (1536 dims, convert WebP to PNG), and, as a control, one local open model (for example DINOv2 or SigLIP on a laptop, no Python sidecar in production) to see whether the hosted models are even in the right ballpark.
3. In a scratch Postgres with pgvector (or in memory), cosine nearest-neighbour search per photo over the 200 catalog vectors. Report top-1 and top-5 accuracy per model, separately for glare and non-glare photos, and the 20 holo cards.
4. Log the measured round-trip latency from Railway for one photo, free-tier rate-limit responses (429s) while embedding the 200 catalog images, and the usage numbers returned (`usage.image_pixels` for Voyage), which settles the UNVERIFIED limits in this note.
5. Decision rule: if a hosted model gets top-5 of at least 90% on the 30 photos, adopt the cheaper-to-run one and scale to the 12k batch. If both fall below that, hosted embeddings are not enough and the self-hosted DINOv2/CLIP sidecar comes back on the table (or a hosted model plus a re-ranking step).
